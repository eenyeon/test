# PainBridge — Grant Roadmap

**Document type:** Standalone strategic document
**Intended uses:** Grant planning, program-officer conversations, aims-page drafting
**Status:** Working plan, v1 (July 2026)

---

## 0. Portfolio logic

The program has two fundable identities that reinforce each other:

1. **Instrument development and validation** (measurement science of pain
   phenotyping and legibility) → NIH HEAL / NINDS / NCCIH / AHRQ.
2. **Health equity in pain communication** (cultural-linguistic stream) →
   NIMHD / NINR / PCORI.

Sequence: R21 (instrument) → R34 (trial planning) + NIMHD or PCORI (equity
stream) → R01 (cluster trial) with SBIR/STTR running in parallel on the
commercial track. Every early study (`05-five-year-research-program.md`,
Studies 1, 3, 5, 7) is designed to be preliminary data for this sequence.

---

## 1. NIH R21 — the first target

- **Framing:** "Development and validation of a patient-generated multimodal
  pain phenotyping instrument to improve the clinical legibility of chronic
  pain."
- **Institute/program fit:** NINDS or NCCIH under the **NIH HEAL
  Initiative** — mechanism-based pain phenotyping and pain-management
  communication are named HEAL priorities. AHRQ (health communication) as
  fallback.
- **Aims:** (1) refine the Phenotype Report via patient/clinician co-design;
  (2) validate report-derived mechanism signals against blinded reference
  clinical assessment; (3) pilot visit-level effects on clinician
  mechanistic concordance and patient felt validation.
- **Innovation language:** clinical legibility as a novel, measurable
  intervention target; the Pain Legibility Cascade as framework; patient-
  generated (not clinician- or system-generated) phenotyping.
- **Preliminary data needed:** clinician discovery interviews; vignette-study
  results; pilot feasibility (N≈30); letters from clinic partner.
- **Reviewer risks → responses:** *"It's a symptom checker"* → explicitly
  non-diagnostic, transparent validated-instrument scoring, no condition
  probabilities. *"Screeners already exist"* → the innovation is the
  legibility construct and the integrated instrument, not the screeners.
  *"Feasibility"* → pilot data + clinic partner letter.

## 2. NIH R34 — trial planning

- **Framing:** planning a cluster-randomized trial of report-supported
  visits vs. usual care in primary care.
- **Fit:** NINDS/HEAL clinical-trial planning mechanisms; NCCIH R34.
- **Aims:** finalize trial protocol and outcome battery (legibility metric,
  mechanistic concordance adjudication, felt validation); site readiness in
  a PBRN; implementation mapping.
- **Preliminary data:** funded/completed R21; pilot RCT signal (Study 8).
- **Risks → responses:** outcome-measure maturity → the R21 delivers the
  legibility metric's psychometrics before R34 submission.

## 3. NIH R01 — the definitive trial

- **Framing:** cluster-randomized trial in primary care; primary outcome
  **mechanistically concordant management at 3 months**; secondary: felt
  validation, PEG interference, low-value care (imaging, opioid initiation
  in nociplastic-pattern patients); mediation analysis follows the PLC
  stages.
- **Fit:** NINDS/HEAL; AHRQ for the low-value-care angle; NIMHD if
  equity-stratified design leads.
- **Risks → responses:** contamination across clinicians → cluster design;
  "communication interventions have small effects" → mechanism-mismatch
  outcomes are utilization-anchored, not just attitudinal.

## 4. Career-development option (if PI-eligible)

- **K23/K01 framing:** "Patient-generated phenotyping to reduce disparities
  in chronic pain assessment" — mentored path combining pain measurement,
  communication science, and equity. Only relevant if an early-career
  clinician-scientist on the team will lead; otherwise skip.

## 5. PCORI

- **Framing:** comparative effectiveness of phenotype-report-supported care
  vs. usual care for **limited-English-proficiency patients with chronic
  pain**; outcomes patients chose (felt validation, being believed, shared
  decision-making, trust).
- **Fit:** PCORI's engagement requirements match the community-advisory
  structure the language stream needs anyway. Consider a PCORI Engagement
  Award first (builds the patient/community partnership cheaply).
- **Preliminary data:** qualitative study (Study 5); community partner MOUs.
- **Risks → responses:** engagement authenticity → community advisory board
  per language group with real governance roles, formed before submission.

## 6. NSF / I-Corps

- **Framing:** I-Corps on the **pain-clinic intake use case** — 100 customer
  discovery interviews: who owns intake pain, what would they pay.
- **Continuation:** NSF PFI (Partnerships for Innovation) once
  product-market signal exists; NSF SBIR as alternative to NIH SBIR if the
  technology framing (NLP/corpus methods) leads.
- **Risks:** NSF wants technology innovation, not clinical validation — lead
  with the language/NLP layer for NSF, the clinical instrument for NIH.

## 7. SBIR/STTR

- **Framing:** "Mechanism-aware digital intake and phenotype reporting for
  pain clinics." Phase I: feasibility + usability with clinic partner;
  Phase II: multi-site deployment + EHR export + validation dossier.
- **Fit:** **NIDA and NINDS HEAL SBIR** programs fund pain-care technology;
  NCCIH SBIR possible.
- **Structure:** STTR pairs the academic lab (corpus, NLP, validation) with
  the company (product) — cleanest given a PI with a university appointment;
  requires forming the small business entity first (see 90-day plan).
- **Risks → responses:** commercialization plausibility → I-Corps interview
  log + clinic letters of intent.

## 8. Foundations and other sources

- **Foundation targets:** pain-focused and equity-focused foundations
  (e.g., Rita Allen Foundation pain awards for early-stage investigators;
  arthritis and rheumatology foundations for the inflammatory-differentiation
  angle; local/health-system foundations for the LEP access angle). Use for
  corpus seed funding — foundations tolerate earlier-stage risk than NIH.
- **Health-system innovation grants:** partner institution's innovation fund
  for the pilot's operational costs; these also signal institutional buy-in
  to NIH reviewers.
- **Institutional:** CTSA pilot awards (KL2/TL1 environment) — fast money for
  Study 3 (vignette) and Study 5 (qualitative).
