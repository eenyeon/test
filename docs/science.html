The scientific model

The Pain Legibility Cascade.

Pain routinely fails to become clinically legible: it is experienced intensely but not recognized, believed, interpreted, documented, or acted upon. PainBridge treats that failure not as background noise, but as a measurable, modifiable target — and the Phenotype Report as an instrument engineered against it.

The cascade

Where pain information gets lost.

Pain information passes through a chain of transitions on its way from body to outcome. At each one, information is lost or distorted — and the losses are systematically patterned by language, culture, stigma, and clinical workflow.

1Lived bodily experience — the pain itself.
↓ lost to: limited pain vocabulary, minimization norms, language barriers
2Expression — words, metaphors, body maps, images.
↓ lost to: idioms with no literal translation, culturally patterned under-reporting
3Cultural & contextual meaning — what the expression means for this person.
↓ lost to: instruments that flatten pain to a 0–10 number
4Multimodal capture — structured, faithful recording across modalities.
↓ lost to: unstructured narrative clinicians can't map to mechanism in 15 minutes
5Clinical legibility — the clinician can reason mechanistically. ← the Phenotype Report lives here
↓ lost to: agenda mismatch, dismissal, suppressed questions
6Diagnostic conversation — the right questions get asked.
↓ lost to: mechanism-mismatched care (imaging, opioids for the wrong pattern)
7Treatment matching & navigation — care fits the mechanism.
8Outcomes — pain, function, trust, appropriate utilization.

PainBridge is engineered to reduce loss at transitions 2→5 — and to measure loss everywhere. That is what distinguishes it from etiological frameworks like the biopsychosocial model: those explain why pain exists and persists; the Pain Legibility Cascade explains why pain that exists fails to be recognized and acted on, and places a designable instrument at the failure point.

Testable claims

Hypotheses, not slogans.

H1 — Legibility

Phenotype-report-supported visits produce more accurate clinician characterization of pain mechanism than usual care, judged against blinded reference assessment.

H2 — Validation

Report use increases patients' felt validation and question-asking — especially for patients with a history of having their pain dismissed.

H3 — Discordance as signal

Divergence between what patients say and what they map (minimal words, extensive body map) marks minimization and predicts diagnostic delay.

H4 — Idioms carry signal

Culturally embedded pain expressions map non-randomly onto mechanistic descriptor space — when clarified through the individual patient, never through a dictionary.

H5 — Less mismatched care

Mechanism-legible presentations reduce mechanism-mismatched care — fewer opioid initiations and imaging orders for widespread, centrally-mediated pain patterns.

Design principles the science demands

Four rules the platform never breaks.

Safety before summary

Deterministic, guideline-based red-flag screening runs before any other module — always. A structured summary is worthless if it delays urgent care.

Signals, never diagnoses

Every pattern is a screening signal with its score shown. No condition probabilities, no treatment directives, no diagnostic nouns aimed at the patient.

Idioms as hypotheses

A cultural expression is a starting point for clarifying questions asked of this patient — never a lookup table about a group. Original words stay on the report, always.

Deterministic where it matters

Scoring uses published, transparent logic. Language models may organize and rephrase within validated templates; they never generate clinical claims.
