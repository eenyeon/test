The problem

Pain is not one thing. And it often doesn't come across.

Pain is the most common reason people seek care — and the most commonly misunderstood. It is invisible, fluctuating, culturally embedded, and hard to put into words. When pain is chronic, widespread, or doesn't match a test result, patients are too often left feeling unheard, and clinicians are left without the information they need to reason about mechanism: is this nerve-related, inflammatory, mechanical, or a change in how the nervous system processes pain?

PainBridge is built around one question: which pain mechanism, pattern, body system, red flag, or care pathway may matter here — and how do we make that visible in the visit?

What usually gets lost
  • Pain quality and path — a 0–10 scale can't carry "burning wire down my leg"
  • The full pain map — people report the worst area and omit the rest
  • Expressions that don't translate — idioms and metaphors flattened by interpretation
  • What was already tried, and what was never tried
  • What the patient most wants understood

Mechanism-aware by design

Different pain mechanisms call for different conversations.

Instead of treating pain as a single symptom, PainBridge organizes what you enter around the mechanism patterns clinicians reason with — as signals to discuss, with the scoring shown transparently.

Nociceptive

Tissue & mechanical

Injury, strain, arthritis, postsurgical pain. Often movement-linked, localized, reproducible.

Neuropathic

Nerve-related

Burning, electric, shooting, tingling — often traveling along a path, as in sciatica or neuropathy.

Nociplastic

Altered pain processing

Widespread pain with fatigue, unrefreshing sleep, brain fog, and sensory sensitivity — as in fibromyalgia.

Inflammatory

Immune-driven

Long morning stiffness, swelling, warmth, redness — patterns that point toward rheumatology.

Visceral

Internal organs

Deep, cramping, pelvic, bladder, bowel, or cycle-linked patterns pointing toward GI, GYN, or urology care.

Mixed

More than one at once

Very common — like back pain with nerve-pattern leg pain plus a widespread ache. PainBridge keeps both visible.

The core artifact

The Pain Phenotype Report.

One page. Readable in under a minute. Every pattern traceable to your actual answers, with the scores shown. Your own words — in your own language — stay on the page, verbatim.

  • Main complaint, story, and your priority — verbatim
  • Body map with pain travel paths
  • Mechanism signals with transparent screening scores
  • Red-flag screen results, stated conservatively
  • Function and what pain prevents
  • Treatment history — including what was never tried
  • Your selected questions, adapted per specialist

Preparation, not prescription

Patterns become better questions.

PainBridge doesn't recommend treatment. It helps you ask mechanism-specific questions about options commonly discussed in clinical care — and flags care that is often not helpful for a pattern, so you can ask before pursuing it.

If your pattern looks…Questions often worth asking about
Nerve-related (burning, electric, traveling) Neurological exam · whether tests like EMG/NCS or imaging fit · medications used for nerve pain
Inflammatory (long stiffness, swelling) Joint exam · inflammation labs · rheumatology evaluation · NSAID safety
Mechanical (movement-linked, localized) Physical therapy · graded activity · ergonomics · when imaging is and isn't useful
Widespread / nociplastic (fatigue, brain fog, sensitivity) Assessment with established criteria · sleep treatment · pacing · pain-focused CBT/ACT · non-opioid options
Visceral (cramping, pelvic, cycle- or meal-linked) GI / GYN / urology pathways · pelvic floor PT · tracking by meals, cycle, bowel, bladder

Safety architecture

Red-flag sensitive. Deliberately non-diagnostic.

Urgent patterns are checked first

Before anything else, PainBridge screens for symptom patterns that can need urgent care — new bladder or bowel problems with back pain, thunderclap headache, new weakness, fever with severe pain, and more. If your answers match one, PainBridge tells you to seek care now, not to keep filling out forms.

And a clear screen is worded honestly: "no urgent flags in what you entered" — never "you're fine."

What PainBridge will never do

No diagnoses. No condition probabilities. No prescriptions or dosages. Every pattern on your report is a signal to discuss, computed with transparent scoring your clinician can check — because your diagnosis belongs in an exam room, made by a clinician who can examine you.

Dismissal protection works the other way too: symptoms don't have to be emergencies to be clinically meaningful.

The science underneath

Built as a research instrument, not a wellness app.

PainBridge is grounded in a testable model — the Pain Legibility Cascade — of how pain information degrades on its way from lived experience to clinical action, and how a structured, culturally responsive phenotype report can stop the loss.
