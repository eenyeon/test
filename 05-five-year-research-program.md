# PainBridge — Five-Year Research Program

**Document type:** Standalone strategic/scientific document
**Intended uses:** Grant program narratives, collaborator recruitment, lab planning
**Status:** Working plan, v1 (July 2026)

---

## 1. Central scientific question

> **Does structured, culturally and linguistically responsive,
> mechanism-aware, patient-generated phenotyping make pain clinically
> legible — and does legibility causally improve diagnostic conversations,
> mechanistically concordant care, and patient outcomes?**

Theoretical frame: the Pain Legibility Cascade (`03-painbridge-conceptual-model.md`).

## 2. Three research streams

**Stream A — Measurement science of legibility.**
Define and validate legibility metrics; psychometrics of the Phenotype
Report (content validity, inter-rater interpretability, concordance with
reference clinical assessment); cross-modal concordance/discordance as
signal.

**Stream B — Cultural-linguistic pain expression.**
Build the multilingual pain-language corpus; idiom/metaphor→descriptor
mapping with bilingual clinical consensus; validation across language pairs.
Start with one pair — Spanish–English maximizes N and funder relevance;
Korean–English maximizes team access/expertise; choose by team strengths.

**Stream C — Clinical translation.**
Visit-level and cluster trials of report-supported care; implementation
science in primary care and pain clinics; equity outcomes.

## 3. Ten publishable studies

| # | Study | Stream | Design | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Pain Legibility Cascade: a framework for pain communication failure | A | Concept paper | Positions the field; doubles as grant framework |
| 2 | Development and content validity of the Pain Phenotype Report | A | Delphi with pain clinicians across specialties | Instrument's foundation paper |
| 3 | Does a phenotype report change clinical reasoning? | A/C | Clinician vignette study: mechanistic characterization with vs. without report | Cheapest, fastest evidence the artifact matters; no software needed |
| 4 | Cross-modal concordance in pain reporting | A | Body map vs. verbal descriptors vs. instrument scores; discordance as minimization marker | Novel signal claim (H3) |
| 5 | The experience of pain illegibility and invalidation across language groups | B | Qualitative, multi-language interviews | Feeds corpus; anchors PCORI engagement |
| 6 | A taxonomy of [Korean/Spanish] pain idioms and their mapping to mechanistic descriptor space | B | Corpus + bilingual clinician consensus | First corpus paper; the moat begins |
| 7 | Feasibility and acceptability of the PainBridge MVP in a pain clinic | C | Single-site pilot, N≈30 | The preliminary-data engine |
| 8 | Report-supported visit vs. usual care | C | Pilot RCT; outcomes = felt validation, communication quality, clinician concordance | First causal evidence (H1, H2) |
| 9 | Automated descriptor extraction from free-text pain narrative | A/B | NLP methods paper, benchmarked against instruments | Methods credibility; enables scale |
| 10 | The cost of illegibility: mechanism-mismatched care patterns | C | Health-services analysis (opioids/imaging in nociplastic-pattern patients) | Anchors payer conversations and R01 significance |

## 4. Sequencing (indicative)

- **Year 1:** Studies 1, 3, 5, 7 (concept paper, vignette study, qualitative, pilot); R21 submitted.
- **Year 2:** Studies 2, 4, 6; R21 funded period begins; PCORI engagement award or NIMHD R21 for Stream B.
- **Year 3:** Studies 8, 9; R34 (trial planning); SBIR Phase I with clinic partner.
- **Year 4:** R01 cluster trial launches; Study 10; corpus expands to second language pair.
- **Year 5:** R01 mid-trial; implementation papers; trials-licensing validation dossier complete.

## 5. First pilot study (6–9 months from now)

Study 7: 30 patients at one partner pain clinic complete the MVP before
intake. Clinicians rate usefulness, time saved, and whether the report
changed their assessment; patients rate acceptability and felt validation;
2- and 6-week follow-up captures care events. Single site, IRB-light,
generates every preliminary-data element the R21 needs.

## 6. Flagship grant-linked studies

- **Best R21** (NINDS/NCCIH, NIH HEAL): develop and validate the phenotyping
  instrument; pilot visit-level effects. (Aims in `06-grant-roadmap.md`.)
- **Best R34→R01:** planning then execution of a **cluster-randomized trial
  in primary care** — report-supported visits vs. usual care; primary
  outcome: mechanistically concordant management at 3 months; secondary:
  felt validation, PEG, low-value care.
- **Best PCORI:** comparative effectiveness of phenotype-report-supported
  care vs. usual care for **limited-English-proficiency patients with
  chronic pain**, patient-centered outcomes (validation, shared
  decision-making, trust), co-designed with community partners.
- **Best NSF/I-Corps continuation:** customer discovery on the pain-clinic
  intake use case (100 interviews), then NSF PFI or SBIR.
- **Best SBIR/STTR:** NIDA or NINDS HEAL SBIR — "Mechanism-aware digital
  intake and phenotype reporting for pain clinics"; Phase I feasibility with
  clinic partner, Phase II multi-site + EHR export. STTR variant pairs the
  lab (corpus/NLP) with the company.

## 7. Ideal collaborators

1. **Pain-medicine or rheumatology clinical champion** — pick before
   anything else; gates the pilot site, the Delphi, and the R21 letters.
2. Psychometrician (legibility metric, report validation).
3. Medical anthropologist / linguist (corpus, Stream B).
4. NLP researcher (Study 9, corpus tooling).
5. Primary-care practice-based research network (PBRN) for the cluster trial.
6. Community organization per language group (PCORI requirement; stereotyping
   safeguard).
7. Biostatistician with cluster-trial experience.

## 8. Data to start collecting now (before any funding)

- 15–20 documented clinician discovery interviews (also commercialization
  evidence).
- Verbatim pain descriptions and idioms from 15–20 patient interviews,
  including ≥5 in the target second language (corpus seed; consented and
  recorded).
- Vignette-study results (Study 3) using mock reports — needs no product.
- Pilot usefulness/acceptability ratings the moment the MVP exists.

All patient-derived data collected under IRB protocols with tiered consent
for research reuse (see `08-data-and-ip-strategy.md`).
